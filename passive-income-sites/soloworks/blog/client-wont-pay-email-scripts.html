Client won't pay? The exact emails to send, week by week

Late payment is almost never malice. It's a buried email, a missing PO number, an approver on holiday — friction, not theft. Which is good news: friction responds to a system. Here is the four-stage sequence, on a fixed schedule, that resolves the overwhelming majority of late invoices without burning the relationship.

Two rules before the scripts: send each email on schedule without waiting to "feel it's time" (the schedule is what keeps it unemotional), and never do new work for a client who is two stages deep in this sequence.

Stage 1 — Due date +1: the friendly nudge

Assume good faith completely. You're helping them, not chasing them.

Subject: Invoice #23 — friendly nudge

Hi Sarah — just flagging that invoice #23 for $2,400 fell due yesterday. Probably just slipped through — I've re-attached it here. Shout if anything's needed on my end for processing (PO number, different format, supplier form — happy to sort it today).

That last clause matters: it removes the "we needed X from you" excuse in advance.

Stage 2 — Day +7: the direct ask

Still warm, but now there's a question that requires an answer.

Subject: Invoice #23 now a week overdue

Hi Sarah — invoice #23 ($2,400) is now 7 days past due. Can you let me know when payment will be made? If there's an issue with the invoice itself or an internal process I should follow, tell me and I'll fix it today.

"When will payment be made" is deliberately not "is everything OK?" — vague questions get vague silence; date questions get dates.

Stage 3 — Day +14: the pause notice

The tone shifts from asking to informing. You are not threatening — you are describing what your terms say happens next, and offering the exit.

Subject: Invoice #23 — 14 days overdue, work pausing

Hi Sarah — invoice #23 remains unpaid 14 days past its due date. Per our agreed terms, I'm pausing work on the project until it's settled, and late fees now apply. I'd much rather keep things moving — a payment date by reply today does that.

Pausing work is the single most effective step in the sequence. A freelancer who keeps delivering while unpaid is telling the client that payment is optional.

Stage 4 — Day +30: the escalation

Copy their accounts/finance address. Name a specific consequence with a specific date. Stay completely flat in tone — anger reads as weakness here.

Subject: Invoice #23 — final notice before escalation

Hi Sarah — despite reminders on 18 June, 25 June and 2 July, invoice #23 for $2,400 is now 30 days overdue. If it isn't paid by 17 July, I'll refer it to a collections service and this becomes a formal debt-recovery matter, which neither of us wants. A payment this week resolves everything.

Beyond this: small-claims court is cheaper and simpler than most freelancers expect for amounts in the low thousands, and a letter from any lawyer often produces payment by itself. Whether it's worth it is a dollars-and-energy call only you can make.

Make next time boring

  • Deposits — 50% up front means a non-payer costs you half a project, not a whole one.
  • Milestone invoicing — never be more than a few weeks of work exposed.
  • Late fees on every invoice — they move you up the payment queue.
  • Stop-work terms in writing — so Stage 3 is enforcement of an agreement, not a new conflict.
