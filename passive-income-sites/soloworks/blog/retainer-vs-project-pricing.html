Retainer vs project pricing: which makes freelancers more money?

Ask ten freelancers and you'll get a religious war: retainer people preach predictable income, project people preach higher effective rates. Both are right — about different stages of a freelance business. Here's the actual trade-off, and the hybrid that most senior freelancers quietly converge on.

The honest comparison

ProjectsRetainers
Effective rateHigher — you're paid for outcomes and speedLower — clients expect a volume discount
Income stabilityFeast and faminePredictable monthly baseline
Sales effortConstant — every project endsSell once, renews monthly
Scope controlClear start and endScope creep is a way of life
Cash flowLumpy (deposit + milestones)Same date every month
RiskA dry pipelineOne client becoming half your income

When projects win

Projects are the right default when you're building reputation and raising rates fast. Every new project is a chance to re-price — if you're improving quickly, being locked into a rate you set eight months ago is expensive. Projects also suit work with natural edges: a website, a brand identity, a migration. Defined deliverable, defined price, clean exit.

When retainers win

Retainers win the moment your problem shifts from "get paid more per hour" to "stop spending 30% of your time selling." One or two anchor retainers covering your fixed costs changes your psychology: you negotiate projects from safety instead of hunger, and clients can hear the difference.

They also fit work that's genuinely ongoing — maintenance, content, marketing ops, advisory. If the client will need you every month anyway, packaging that as a retainer beats re-scoping tiny projects forever.

The three retainer traps

  • The hour-bank trap. "20 hours/month" invites hour-counting arguments and rollover demands. Sell a capacity and scope ("up to two active requests, 3-business-day turnaround"), not a pile of hours.
  • The discount trap. A retainer deserves maybe 10–15% off your project-equivalent rate for the predictability — not the 40% clients open with. Predictability is worth something to you, but availability is worth more to them.
  • The forever-rate trap. Retainers renew silently, and silent renewal means your rate quietly ages. Put an annual review date in the agreement on day one.

The hybrid most seniors land on

Project-first, retainer-second: sell the big project at full price, then convert the relationship into a smaller ongoing retainer — "post-launch support and iteration, $X/month." The project pays for depth of context; the retainer monetizes it. Conversion line that works:

"Now that I know your stack/brand/codebase, keeping me on for [scope] costs a fraction of re-explaining it to someone new every time something comes up. Here's what a monthly arrangement looks like."

Aim for a floor, not a ceiling: retainers covering 40–60% of your income target, projects on top. Past ~70% retainer income you've reinvented having a job, minus the benefits.
