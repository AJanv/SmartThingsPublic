Getting paid

How to write a freelance invoice (that gets paid on time)

A freelance invoice has one job: remove every possible excuse for not paying you this week. Most invoices fail that job in small, fixable ways — a missing purchase order number, no due date, bank details the client has to email you for. Here's the complete checklist, and what to put in each part.

The 9 things every invoice needs

  1. The word "INVOICE" — big, at the top. Accounts teams triage by scanning; make yours unmistakable.
  2. A unique invoice number — sequential (INV-001, INV-002…). Required for your taxes, and it gives every chase-up email a handle: "invoice #23".
  3. Your full details — legal name or business name, address, email, and tax ID (ABN, EIN, VAT number — whatever applies in your country).
  4. The client's details — and if they gave you a purchase order number, include it. In larger companies, a missing PO number is the #1 cause of "stuck" invoices.
  5. Issue date and due date — both spelled out. "Due on receipt" is polite fiction; "Due 17 July 2026" is a deadline.
  6. Line items that mirror your proposal — same names, same milestones. The approver should recognize every line without asking anyone.
  7. The math, done for them — subtotal, tax (with the rate shown), total due, currency stated explicitly.
  8. Exactly how to pay — bank details or a payment link on the invoice itself. Every "what's your account number?" email is 2–5 days of delay.
  9. Payment terms — the fine print that changes behavior (next section).

Payment terms that actually work

  • 14 days, not 30. Net-30 is a convention from paper-check corporate purchasing, not a law of nature. For small-to-mid clients, 14 days is normal and reasonable. (Enterprise clients will impose their own terms — price the wait in.)
  • Late fee: 1.5% per month. You'll almost never collect it. Its purpose is psychological: invoices with late fees get prioritized over invoices without them.
  • Deposits up front. 50% before work starts on projects. It filters out the clients who were never going to pay well, before they can hurt you.
  • Deliverables transfer on final payment. Source files, exports, and handover happen when the last invoice clears. Stated kindly, enforced always.

When to send it

The same day the work ships. Every day between "delivered" and "invoiced" tells the client this isn't urgent for you either. For retainers, invoice the same date every month — in advance, not arrears. For projects, invoice each milestone the day it's approved rather than saving it all for the end.
