The freelance contract checklist: 12 clauses that save you

Published July 2026

Not legal advice. Contract law differs by country and state. Use this checklist to have a smarter, cheaper conversation with a qualified lawyer — not instead of one.

Most freelance disasters aren't caused by bad clients — they're caused by contracts that never addressed the situation everyone swore wouldn't happen. Every clause below exists because it regularly does. Before you sign (or send) anything, check for these twelve.

Money clauses

  1. Deposit before work. A 50% deposit that books your time and is non-refundable once work begins. This clause filters bad clients out before they can hurt you.
  2. Payment schedule tied to milestones, never only "on completion." "Completion" is a moving target controlled by the other side. Dates and milestones aren't.
  3. Late-payment consequences. A payment window (14 days), a late fee (1.5%/month), and — the one with teeth — the right to pause work on overdue accounts.
  4. A kill fee. If the project is cancelled mid-flight: payment for all work done plus a percentage of the remainder. Cancellation "for convenience" with no kill fee means your schedule was free to waste.
  5. A dormancy clause. If the client goes silent for 30+ days, outstanding milestones are invoiced and restarting is scheduled as new work. Ghost-proofing, in writing.

Work clauses

  1. Deliverables as countable things. "5-page website" survives arguments; "a great new web presence" doesn't. Exclusions listed explicitly.
  2. A revision cap with a definition. "Two rounds, where a round is one consolidated set of change requests submitted together." Without the second half, one round lasts forever.
  3. Client obligations with consequences. Their feedback windows and asset deadlines go in the contract, and their delays shift your deadline day-for-day. Timelines fail from the client side more often than yours.

Protection clauses

  1. IP transfers on full payment — not before. Until the last invoice clears, the work is yours. This is your single greatest piece of leverage; client paper that says IP transfers "on creation" removes it.
  2. Your tools stay yours. Carve out your pre-existing code, templates, and processes ("background IP") from any assignment — otherwise a broad "all work product" clause can sweep up things you reuse across every client.
  3. Portfolio rights. The right to show the work and name the client, unless they request confidentiality in writing. Your future marketing depends on this clause existing.
  4. A liability cap. Your total liability limited to fees paid, excluding indirect losses. Without it, a $2,000 project can theoretically carry a $200,000 problem.

Red flags in client contracts

When the client sends their paper, scan for: unlimited revisions "until satisfied" · net-60/90 payment terms · one-way indemnification · non-competes covering their whole industry · "time is of the essence" while their own delays go unpunished · IP transfer on creation. Each is negotiable — but only if you spot it before signing.
